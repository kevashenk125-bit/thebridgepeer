Experience, across the whole range

My peer work doesn’t sit in one setting. It runs from recovery programs and community support to the street corner where a crisis is happening right now - and every step between.

Treatment access & MAT support

Helping people connect to medication-assisted treatment and recovery care - advocating for real treatment access and supporting them through the first steps.

Systems advocacy

Standing with people inside the systems that decide so much of their lives - treatment, courts, benefits, and care - and pushing for equity, safety, and accountability.

Harm reduction & care coordination

Mobile harm reduction and hands-on care coordination in community-based behavioral health across Central and Western New York.

Boots on the ground

Crisis outreach where it actually happens - on the street, in the moment, person to person. De-escalation, Narcan, and staying until someone is safe.

Why “bridge”?

Because helping people across is simply who I am - and it always has been.

“Helping others has always made me feel good - it’s who I’ve been since childhood.”

I believe in human compassion, honesty, and integrity - treating people with real care, telling the truth, and doing right by them, every time. Those aren’t slogans to me; they’re how I live and how I work.

That’s the whole philosophy. Peer work isn’t paperwork and it isn’t pity. It’s standing on the bridge with somebody until they can cross it themselves - through recovery, through a job change, through the systems that decide so much of a person’s life.

It’s also why my design work aims at the same people: the small, new, and vulnerable businesses that don’t know where to start. Everybody deserves a way across.
